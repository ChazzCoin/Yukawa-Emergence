\section{Beyond the relational class: other sources of orientation}\label{sec:r2}
%=======================================================================
The no-go and the battery of Sec.~\ref{sec:constructive} show that nothing built from $(\He,\Hnu)$,
and no added flavor dimension, orients the $\nu_2$--$\nu_3$ frame. This section tests sources that
are \emph{other data} or \emph{input-free}, together with the hypothesis that nothing needs
explaining. The criteria are (a)--(e) of Sec.~\ref{sec:conditions}, with (b) widened: oriented by
data other than $\theta_{23}$, \emph{or by no continuous input at all}. Every candidate is scored
on two questions: does it give near-maximal mixing, and which octant does it pick?

\subsection{What is established: near-maximal, not the octant}\label{sec:octant}
% From theorist-4 (crew folder files/R2-theorist.md); every number from analysis/data/nufit6_ranges.csv.
NuFIT~6.0~\cite{nufit6} gives, for normal ordering, $\theta_{23}=48.5^{+0.7}_{-0.9}{}^\circ$ (IC19,
without SK-atm) and $43.3^{+1.0}_{-0.8}{}^\circ$ (IC24, with SK-atm); for inverted ordering,
$48.6^{+0.7}_{-0.9}{}^\circ$ and $47.9^{+0.7}_{-0.9}{}^\circ$. The two data sets put the
normal-ordering best fit in opposite octants, and the preferred octant depends on the ordering as
well. In IC19 the inverted ordering is the global best fit and normal ordering sits at
$\Delta\chi^2=0.6$. In IC24 normal ordering is the best fit and inverted ordering sits at
$\Delta\chi^2=6.1$. In units of the side-appropriate $1\sigma$ error,
$45^\circ$ lies $3.9\sigma$ and $1.7\sigma$ (NO) and $4.0\sigma$ and $3.2\sigma$ (IO) from the best
fits. But the $\chi^2$ profile is not Gaussian, and every published $3\sigma$ interval
($[41.0,50.5]^\circ$ and $[41.3,49.9]^\circ$ NO; $[41.4,50.6]^\circ$ and $[41.5,49.8]^\circ$ IO)
contains $45^\circ$. Read off the published one-dimensional profiles (Fig.~1 of
Ref.~\cite{nufit6}, digitised to $\pm0.3$), maximal mixing costs $\Delta\chi^2\approx3.8$ and $2.2$
(NO, IC19 and IC24) and $5.7$ and $2.8$ (IO), each relative to its own ordering's minimum, i.e.\
roughly $1.5$--$2.4\sigma$. What the data establish is that $\theta_{23}$ is near-maximal: the best fits lie
within $3.6^\circ$ of $45^\circ$, and the $3\sigma$ ranges within $[41.0^\circ,50.6^\circ]$. The octant,
and whether $\theta_{23}$ differs from $45^\circ$ at all, are not established. Each mechanism below
is therefore scored on two questions: does it give near-maximal $\theta_{23}$, and which octant does
it pick. The octant a candidate picks is reported as its prediction, without a pass or fail, until
the per-variant $\Delta\chi^2$ between the octants is in hand.

\subsection{D7: residual-symmetry sum rules}\label{sec:D7}
\emph{Candidate.} TM1, TM2 and $\mu$--$\tau$ reflection, which give closed-form relations between
$\theta_{23}$, $\theta_{13}$, $\delta$ (and $\theta_{12}$), plus the general first-order rules
$\theta_{23}-45^\circ\sim\theta_{13}\cos\delta$.\\
\emph{Gate.} Reproduce each rule against its source (exact forms: item \#12).\\
\emph{Question.} With $\theta_{13}$ and $\delta$ at NuFIT~6.0 (central values and $3\sigma$
$\delta$ ranges, all four variants), what $\theta_{23}$ and octant does each rule give, and is the
result inside the $3\sigma$ window? \emph{Null.} The window is about $10^\circ$ wide, so the score
is the joint prediction ($\theta_{12}$ too, where the rule fixes it) and the sign of the
$\delta$--octant correlation. (b) passes by construction; the test is (e).\\
% Lab: crew/flavor-obstruction-lab 3ffb5ed, calculations/theta23-sum-rules/ (README; captures named inline).
\emph{Result.} Line (b), $\theta_{23}(\delta)$, is scored on the Valencia 2020 two-dimensional $\chi^2$
grids (Valencia 2020, not NuFIT~6.0; the NuFIT 2D grids were not obtained). Under the registered
statistic with the full trial count ($\times27\times2$), two cells pass: TM1 (IO), $p=0.005$, and
Petcov GRA (IO), $p=0.016$. The curve control asks how often a curve of the same shape with a
different constant scores as well. It gives fractions $0.520$, $0.130$, $0.142$ and $0.681$ (TM1 NO,
TM2 NO, TM1 IO, TM2 IO), none below the $0.005$ threshold, so no TM cell is special on the control
(\texttt{run-2026-10-23-d7-recount.txt}). Line (a), $\theta_{12}(\theta_{13})$, is scored on
NuFIT~6.0 $1\sigma$ errors with $\theta_{13}$ propagated. TM1 sits $+0.85\sigma$ to $+0.95\sigma$
from the data, not special after multiplicity; TM2 sits $+2.74\sigma$ to $+2.82\sigma$, inside
$3\sigma$ (\texttt{run-2026-10-23-d7-line-a-nufit.txt}).\quad
\emph{Verdict.} No sum rule is special on $\theta_{23}(\delta)$ under the curve control. The
$\theta_{23}$ verdicts rest on Valencia 2020 grids and are provisional until a NuFIT~6.0 rerun.


\subsection{D8: quark--lepton complementarity}\label{sec:D8}
\emph{Candidate.} $U_{\rm PMNS}=U_{\rm bimax}V_{\rm CKM}^\dagger$ (and the other order):
$\theta_{12}+\theta_C\simeq45^\circ$, $\theta_{23}+\theta_{23}^{\rm CKM}\simeq45^\circ$.\\
\emph{Gate.} Reproduce Minakata--Smirnov and Raidal (2004) with PDG CKM inputs (item \#12).\\
\emph{Question.} The exact $\theta_{23}$ (and $\theta_{12}$, $\theta_{13}$, $\delta$) for each
composition order and phase convention, against NuFIT; the octant each order picks; whether any
order gets $\theta_{13}$ right. \emph{Null.} Random correction matrices with the CKM's angle
sizes.\\
% Lab: calculations/theta23-sum-rules/ (D8 PART), capture run-2026-10-23-d7-line-a-nufit.txt.
\emph{Result.} In both composition orders $\theta_{23}$ enters as the bimaximal input, so the
ansatz makes no $\theta_{23}$ prediction to score; it is tested on $(\sin^2\theta_{12},\sin^2\theta_{13})$
against NuFIT~6.0. Order~2 gives $\sin^2\theta_{13}=0.00056$, $\chi^2=1366$--$1515$ (2 dof): excluded.
Order~1, with the phase $g$ fitted free ($g=5.89$), gives $\chi^2=8.90$--$12.69$ (2 dof) across the
four variants, roughly $2\sigma$ tension (\texttt{run-2026-10-23-d7-line-a-nufit.txt}). Its small
family-wise number against Haar is flagged but not called special, because a Haar null rewards any
small-$\theta_{13}$ ansatz and $g$ is an unpenalised fit parameter (lab README, record edits).\quad
\emph{Verdict.} $\theta_{23}$ is an input by ansatz, not an output. Order~2 is excluded; order~1 is
not special.


\subsection{D9: Haar typicality (anarchy)}\label{sec:D9}
\emph{Candidate.} No mechanism: the mixing matrix is a typical Haar draw on $U(3)$.\\
\emph{Gate.} Reproduce de~Gouv\^ea--Murayama: the flat Haar-induced variables and their reported
p-value (item \#12).\\
\emph{Question.} The p-value of the NuFIT~6.0 point under Haar, and the Bayes factor of Haar
against a $\mu$--$\tau$-reflection-centred prior (width from the D2 breaking size). If Haar is not
disfavoured, the verdict is that no orientation mechanism is required by the data.\\
% Lab: calculations/theta23-anarchy/ (README; captures named inline).
\emph{Result.} On the de~Gouv\^ea--Murayama statistic, the NuFIT~6.0 best fits give
$p_H=0.410$, $0.427$, $0.412$ and $0.419$ (NO IC19, NO IC24, IO IC19, IO IC24;
\texttt{run-2026-10-23-scored.txt}), and over the full $3\sigma$ box $p_H$ ranges over
$0.353$--$0.488$ in every variant (\texttt{run-2026-10-23-box.txt}). The statistic cannot detect
closeness to $45^\circ$: setting $\theta_{23}=45^\circ$ exactly gives $p_H=0.439$--$0.443$, a
\emph{more} typical value, and the box maximum sits at $\theta_{23}=45^\circ$. As information only
(post hoc, not a registered trial): the Haar probability of being at least this close to maximal,
$2|\sin^2\theta_{23}-\tfrac12|$, is $0.122$, $0.059$, $0.125$ and $0.101$ in the same order
(\texttt{run-2026-10-23-challenge.txt}). A Bayes factor against a reflection-centred prior was
computed only on the Valencia 2020 grids (Valencia 2020, not NuFIT~6.0) and is kept out of the
verdict.\quad
\emph{Verdict.} Haar is not disfavoured anywhere in the $3\sigma$ box. The test is blind to the
feature R2 asks about, so ``not disfavoured'' does not show that near-maximal $\theta_{23}$ needs no
explanation.


\subsection{D10: modular symmetry at fixed points}\label{sec:D10}
\emph{Candidate.} An $A_4$ modular model at the fixed points $\tau=i,\ \omega,\ i\infty$
(residual $\mathbb Z_2$, $\mathbb Z_3$, $\mathbb Z_3^T$), with no continuous input.\\
\emph{Gate.} Reproduce one published best fit: the weight-2 level-3 forms $Y_i(\tau)$, the mass
matrices, and the angles at the published $\tau$ (item \#12).\\
\emph{Question.} The angles at each fixed point: near-maximal $\theta_{23}$, right $\theta_{12}$
and $\theta_{13}$, which octant. \emph{Null.} The angle distribution over random $\tau$ in the
fundamental domain.\\
% Lab: crew/flavor-obstruction-lab, calculations/theta23-modular-fixed-points/ (gate c94252c, capture
% run-2026-10-23.txt; independent reproduction e25408b, capture run-2026-10-23-validate.txt). Literature:
% crew folder files/R2-D10-literature.md. Only A4 level 3 at tau = i was computed.
\emph{Result.} The gate asks two questions at $\tau=i$: (G1) does any continuous coupling left at the
fixed point enter $\theta_{23}$, and (G2) is $\tau=i$ forced by the modulus potential? The lab computed
Feruglio's three $A_4$ level-3 examples~\cite{feruglio2017} from the weight-2 forms; the closed forms
agree to $10^{-16}$, and an independent cubic-theta route reproduces them
(\texttt{run-2026-10-23.txt}, \texttt{run-2026-10-23-validate.txt}).
(G1) In the Weinberg-operator model, $m_\nu$ at $\tau=i$ is fixed by $\tau$ alone up to an overall
scale, so no coupling enters $\theta_{23}$. The residual symmetry fixes
$\sin^2\theta_{23}=(16-4\sqrt3)/26$, i.e.\ $\theta_{23}=36.2^\circ$ or $53.8^\circ$ by labelling, with
$\theta_{12}=8.8^\circ$ and a degenerate-pair spectrum $|m_1|=|m_2|$, $m_3=0$. Both values lie outside
all four NuFIT~6.0 $3\sigma$ windows. The seesaw examples do not give a physical alternative at
$\tau=i$. In Example~2 the coupling $g$ enters only as an overall scale, and $\sin^2\theta_{23}$ is
the same $0.651/0.349$ for every $g$ tested, with two massless states. In Example~3 the spectrum is
rank one for $g_2/g_1\neq0$, so its angles are not physical (\texttt{run-2026-10-23.txt}).
(G2) In the modulus potential of Ref.~\cite{npp2022}, built from
$H(\tau)=(j-1728)^{m/2}j^{n/3}P(j)$, $\tau=i$ is a minimum only inside a chosen discrete class
($n\neq0$, with $P(j)=1$ taken as the simplest choice); $(m,n,P)$ are free input. The same paper
states that stabilising $\tau$ near $i$, ``even if possible, cannot offer an explanation of these mass
hierarchies'' (there, the charged-lepton and quark mass hierarchies). The $S_4$ model with generalised CP~\cite{nppt2019} fits $\sin^2\theta_{23}=0.4862$, inside
the windows. Its $\tau=\pm0.09922+1.016i$ is a continuous fit variable, not the fixed point. The $\pm$ is the
gCP pair $(\tau,-\tau^*)$, which returns $\delta/\pi=\pm1.641$, i.e.\ $\delta\simeq295^\circ$ and
$\simeq65^\circ$, as equally valid, so the fit cannot pick the sign of $\delta$ that D11 leaves open.
Landing in a window is not a verdict under the family-wise rule. At CP-conserving $\tau$ (the imaginary
axis and the boundary arc, which include the fixed points) the same paper gives real mass matrices
and $\sin\delta=0$.\quad
\emph{Verdict.} D10 closes at the gate under the pre-registered rule. The one model with no
continuous input on $\theta_{23}$ fixes it outside the data, and a vacuum at $\tau=i$ is a chosen class of
the potential, not forced by it. A generalised-CP model at a fixed point gives $\delta=0$ or $\pi$.


\subsection{D11: an extremum principle}\label{sec:D11}
\emph{Candidate.} Maximal CP violation:
$J=s_{12}c_{12}s_{13}c_{13}^2s_{23}c_{23}\sin\delta$ is extremal in $\theta_{23}$ at $45^\circ$ and
in $\delta$ at $\pm90^\circ$, so a maximal-$J$ principle gives the $\mu$--$\tau$-reflection pattern
without a symmetry.\\
% FINAL ON PAPER (manager, 2026-10-23): proposition and proof from theorist-4 (files/R2-theorist.md), exact
% algebra. Only curvature values at the data points, if quoted, wait on a lab capture.
\begin{proposition}[Maximal CP violation is octant-blind]\label{prop:Jextremum}
Let $J=A\sin2\theta_{23}\sin\delta$ with $A=\tfrac18\sin2\theta_{12}\sin2\theta_{13}\cos\theta_{13}>0$.
(i) On $\theta_{23}\in(0,\pi/2)$ the critical points of $J$ are exactly $(\pi/4,\pm\pi/2)$.
(ii) For every fixed $\delta$ with $\sin\delta\neq0$, $\partial_{\theta_{23}}J=0$ iff
$\theta_{23}=\pi/4$.
(iii) $J(\pi/2-\theta_{23},\delta)=J(\theta_{23},\delta)$ identically, so no function of $J$
distinguishes the octants, at any order.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\partial_{\theta_{23}}J=2A\cos2\theta_{23}\sin\delta$ and $\partial_\delta J=A\sin2\theta_{23}\cos\delta$.
On the open interval $\sin2\theta_{23}\neq0$, which gives (i) and (ii). (iii) holds because
$\sin2(\pi/2-\theta_{23})=\sin2\theta_{23}$.
\end{proof}
\noindent Near $(45^\circ,-90^\circ)$, with $x=\theta_{23}-45^\circ$ and $y=\delta+90^\circ$,
$J=-A\cos2x\cos y=-A+2Ax^2+\tfrac{A}{2}y^2+O(4)$, with no cross term. An offset in $\delta$
therefore induces no shift in $\theta_{23}$; only the width depends on $\delta$, through the
$\theta_{23}$-curvature $4A|\sin\delta|$. The same holds verbatim for the weak-basis CP-odd invariant
$\Tr([\He,\Hnu]^3)\propto J$. The $e$-row Majorana invariants $\operatorname{Im}(U_{e1}U_{e3}^*)$ and
$\operatorname{Im}(U_{e2}U_{e3}^*)$ do not depend on $\theta_{23}$ at all.\\
\emph{A CP-even exception (leading order, under test).} The misalignment norm is not octant-blind.
To leading order in $m_\mu^2/m_\tau^2$ and $\Delta m^2_{21}/\Delta m^2_{31}$,
$-\tfrac12\Tr([\He,\Hnu]^2)=m_\tau^4\,|U_{\tau3}|^2(1-|U_{\tau3}|^2)\,(\Delta m^2_{31})^2$, which is
maximal at $|U_{\tau3}|^2=\tfrac12$, i.e.\ $\theta_{23}^*\approx44.4^\circ$ at
$\sin^2\theta_{13}=0.022$ (first octant, independent of $\delta$ at this order). [pending: lab check
of the exact argmax, theorist-4's C2]\\
\emph{Question (lab).} With $\theta_{12}$ and $\theta_{13}$ at data, what deviation from $45^\circ$
does the principle allow once $\delta$ is at its data value; and is any other natural invariant
extremal at the data point? \emph{Null.} Random points.\\
\emph{Result.} By Proposition~\ref{prop:Jextremum}, $J$ and every function of it are octant-blind at
all orders. The extremum principle's other prediction, $\delta=\pm90^\circ$, splits: $\delta=270^\circ$
lies inside all four NuFIT~6.0 $3\sigma$ ranges, and $\delta=90^\circ$ lies outside all four
(the normal-ordering ranges $96^\circ\to422^\circ$ and $124^\circ\to364^\circ$ wrap past $360^\circ$
only to $62^\circ$ and $4^\circ$; \texttt{analysis/data/nufit6\_ranges.csv}).\quad
\emph{Verdict.} Maximal CP violation gives near-maximal $\theta_{23}$ and $\delta=270^\circ$, both
allowed, but it cannot pick an octant. The CP-even exception above is still pending.

\subsection{D12: the Littlest Seesaw}\label{sec:D12}
% Lab: calculations/theta23-littlest-seesaw/ (README; gate run-2026-10-23.txt, rerun run-2026-10-24-scored2.txt,
% reproduction run-2026-10-24-validate.txt; record fix 3ffb5ed).
\emph{Candidate.} Two right-handed neutrinos with constrained sequential dominance CSD($n$):
atmospheric column $\propto(0,1,1)$, solar column fixed by the integer $n$, with the continuous inputs fixed
by observables other than $\theta_{23}$. The gate reproduces the published LS predictions
(closed forms to $4.4\times10^{-13}$; \texttt{run-2026-10-23.txt}).\\
\emph{Result.} With $\theta_{23}$ removed from the $\chi^2$ and the $\delta$ term built piecewise from
the NuFIT~6.0 $1\sigma$ and $3\sigma$ edges, $n=3$ fits well: $\chi^2=3.63$ (NO IC19) and $3.18$
(NO IC24) at $\delta\simeq271.7^\circ$, predicting $\theta_{23}=45.38^\circ$ and $45.41^\circ$, inside both
normal-ordering windows (\texttt{run-2026-10-24-scored2.txt}). Inverted ordering is not fittable
(normal-ordering prediction by construction). The test is a conditional null: among null models that
fit at least as well, how often does $\theta_{23}$ land in the window? Random vectors do so about
$0.15$ of the time (validator, \texttt{run-2026-10-24-validate.txt}); vectors in the CSD plane
(atmospheric $(0,1,1)$, solar $(1,a,b)$) do so $0.36$--$0.47$ of the time (58--121 matched models over both
variants; \texttt{run-2026-10-24-scored2.txt}).\quad
\emph{Verdict.} $n=3$ is the best-fitting R2 candidate, but its near-maximal $\theta_{23}$ is what
the whole CSD plane gives. The CSD($n$) constraint carries no $\theta_{23}$ specialness beyond the
chosen vacuum alignment, and (b) holds only in the sense that the alignment itself is an input.
